# GrowthPilot AI — Research Audit

## Scope and evidence boundary

This is a desk-research snapshot, not a complete competitor audit. Official NIAT/NxtWave and Scaler pages could not be retrieved in the research environment, so their current program details, audience, eligibility, differentiators, and enquiry journeys remain **unverified**. Do not use this document as confirmation of current institutional claims; validate those points with the institution before publication.

## Opportunity hypothesis

**Test a useful technology-path exploration before asking a prospective student to enquire.** The available evidence shows that category messaging emphasizes industry-oriented education (Newton) and that at least one published student story describes needing guidance while exploring software paths (Masai). A short interest/experience/stage assessment that returns practical beginner project ideas could demonstrate value at the point of uncertainty, then offer a clearly optional, consent-based follow-up.

This is a product hypothesis, not proof of an unmet market need or a claim that competitors lack similar tools. The GrowthPilot prototype implements this flow; no conversion lift or acquisition impact has yet been measured.

## Validation before scaling

1. Verify NIAT's current programs, target segments, admissions journey, official claims, and existing enquiry/assessment tools with the institution and its official materials.
2. Audit current Scaler and other relevant competitor flows directly; record date, exact CTA, eligibility, pricing if shown, and source URL.
3. Interview prospective students about where they feel uncertain when choosing a technology pathway.
4. Run a consent-reviewed pilot with one defined traffic source. Compare assessment starts, completions, and consented leads against a baseline; report sample sizes and time window.
5. Do not interpret assessments as aptitude, admission eligibility, or career/employment predictions.
